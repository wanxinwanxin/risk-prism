riskprism

An open risk model for US equities

riskprism answers one question about any stock portfolio: how much should it move, and why? It's a Barra-style factor risk model — the kind of tool quant funds license commercially — built fully in the open: … US stocks, … factors, re-estimated every week from public prices and SEC filings, with every forecast graded publicly against what actually happened. Does the model tell the truth?

Every historical week, the model forecast next-week volatility for a panel of test portfolios — using only information available at the time — and was scored against what actually happened: z = realized return / forecast vol. If the risk numbers are honest, z is standard normal: its standard deviation (the bias statistic) should be ~1.0, and |z| > 1.96 should happen ~5% of the time.

Could a simpler public method have done as well?

The commercial models can't sit in this table — their forecasts are proprietary and their licenses forbid published benchmarks. What can sit here is every classic public-methodology forecaster, replayed point-in-time over the same test portfolios and the same weeks, graded with the same statistics on one shared sample. One asymmetry to keep in mind: each baseline needs that portfolio's own trailing return history, while the factor model prices any weight vector cold — that coverage is the product; matching the univariate baselines on their home turf is the calibration bar.

Against the commercial models — on their own protocols

The vendors can't be forced onto one standard, so we conform to theirs: each row runs this model under that vendor's published test protocol and puts the result next to what the vendor reports about itself. Periods and universes still differ — that caveat can't be engineered away with public data — but the statistic, horizon, and portfolio construction match.

The shared failure modes are telling: commercial research documents that optimized and extreme-tilt portfolios are underforecast (Shepard 2009 — compare our style spreads) and that models lag volatility regime shifts — USE4's answers (Volatility Regime Adjustment, eigenfactor adjustment) are the natural v0.3 roadmap here. One difference stands: this validation history is public, reproducible, and regenerated weekly, not a whitepaper snapshot. Sources: Menchero, Orr & Wang, The Barra US Equity Model (USE4), Methodology & Empirical Notes, MSCI Model Insight, 2011 · Axioma US Equity Factor Risk Models factsheet, 2016 · Bloomberg MAC3.

One week of the model

Every Friday, that week's stock returns are regressed on the exposures known at the start of the week:

ri  =  fmkt  +  Σs Xis·fs  +  Σj Iij·fj  +  εi
  • Weighted least squares with √(market cap) weights — large caps have less noisy returns, so they anchor the fit.
  • Identification constraint: cap-weighted industry factor returns sum to zero, so the intercept fmkt is the cap-weighted market return, and styles/industries are deltas around it.
  • The regression's coefficients are that week's factor returns; what it can't explain (ε) becomes each stock's specific return.

The seven styles

Raw descriptors from prices + SEC EDGAR fundamentals, winsorized at ±3σ, standardized to cap-weighted mean 0 / std 1. A stock's exposure answers: how many standard deviations from the market is it on this dimension?

From weekly returns to annual risk

Factor covariance is an exponentially weighted average of factor-return history. Volatilities react fast (13-week half-life); correlations move slowly (26 weeks):

Assembled risk: Σ = X F Xᵀ + diag(s²) — factor covariance spread through exposures, plus each stock's specific variance (EWMA of its residuals, blended with a structural cross-sectional model by history length). F is repaired to positive semi-definite by eigenvalue flooring.

Read this before trusting it

  • Survivorship bias, decaying — and measured: history recorded before launch excludes names that had already delisted. Audited against SEC bulk archives (which retain dead filers): ~8%/yr of active filers ceased during our window, but they skew small — median last book equity $115M vs $553M for survivors, ~8.7% of filer book equity in total — bounding the cap-weighted return-mean bias at ~1.4–2.5bp/week and covariances at second order. Weekly builds append history with imputed delisting returns, so with 13/26-week half-lives the biased cold start washes out within ~18–24 months.
  • Prior-driven names: stocks outside the estimation universe (illiquid, recent IPOs) get specific risk from a structural model of their characteristics, not their own history — the stock page shows how much is prior.
  • Crude universe heuristics: ticker-pattern filters; some ADRs leak through; one share class per company.
  • First-order stress tests: exposure × shock, no convexity, no specific returns.
  • Full methodology and every parameter: docs/METHODOLOGY.md on GitHub.

The model in one paragraph

Barra-style fundamental factor model: 7 style factors (size, value, momentum, volatility, liquidity, quality, leverage) + 12 Fama-French industries + a market factor, estimated by weekly cross-sectional WLS regressions. Fundamentals and industries come from SEC EDGAR (public domain, point-in-time). Factor covariance is EWMA (13-week vol / 26-week correlation half-lives); specific risk blends per-stock residual EWMA with a structural characteristics model. Everything here is computed in your browser from the published artifacts — no server.
