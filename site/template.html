Does the model tell the truth?

Every historical week, the model forecast next-week volatility for a panel of test portfolios — using only information available at the time — and was scored against what actually happened: z = realized return / forecast vol. If the risk numbers are honest, z is standard normal: its standard deviation (the bias statistic) should be ~1.0, and |z| > 1.96 should happen ~5% of the time.

How the commercial models report this

The bias statistic used here is the industry-standard metric: MSCI Barra's published USE4 model research reports the same realized/forecast ratio, targeting ~1.0, with a documented ±2/√(2n) confidence band. Direct numerical comparison isn't apples-to-apples (different periods, universes, horizons), but the failure modes are tellingly shared: commercial research documents that optimized and extreme-tilt portfolios are underforecast (Shepard 2009 — compare our style spreads at 1.2–1.4) and that models lag volatility regime shifts (low-vol eigenfactors realized ~40% above forecast in pre-USE4 models — compare our Mincer–Zarnowitz slope of 0.7). USE4's answers — the Volatility Regime Adjustment and eigenfactor adjustment — are the natural v0.3 roadmap here, with one difference: our validation history is public and regenerated weekly, not a whitepaper snapshot.

One week of the model

Every Friday, that week's stock returns are regressed on the exposures known at the start of the week:

ri  =  fmkt  +  Σs Xis·fs  +  Σj Iij·fj  +  εi
  • Weighted least squares with √(market cap) weights — large caps have less noisy returns, so they anchor the fit.
  • Identification constraint: cap-weighted industry factor returns sum to zero, so the intercept fmkt is the cap-weighted market return, and styles/industries are deltas around it.
  • The regression's coefficients are that week's factor returns; what it can't explain (ε) becomes each stock's specific return.

The seven styles

Raw descriptors from prices + SEC EDGAR fundamentals, winsorized at ±3σ, standardized to cap-weighted mean 0 / std 1. A stock's exposure answers: how many standard deviations from the market is it on this dimension?

From weekly returns to annual risk

Factor covariance is an exponentially weighted average of factor-return history. Volatilities react fast (13-week half-life); correlations move slowly (26 weeks):

Assembled risk: Σ = X F Xᵀ + diag(s²) — factor covariance spread through exposures, plus each stock's specific variance (EWMA of its residuals, shrunk 30% toward its size-quintile mean). F is repaired to positive semi-definite by eigenvalue flooring.

Read this before trusting it

  • Survivorship bias, decaying — and measured: history recorded before launch excludes names that had already delisted. Audited against SEC bulk archives (which retain dead filers): ~8%/yr of active filers ceased during our window, but they skew small — median last book equity $115M vs $553M for survivors, ~8.7% of filer book equity in total — bounding the cap-weighted return-mean bias at ~1.4–2.5bp/week and covariances at second order. Weekly builds append history with imputed delisting returns, so with 13/26-week half-lives the biased cold start washes out within ~18–24 months.
  • Prior-driven names: stocks outside the estimation universe (illiquid, recent IPOs) get specific risk from a structural model of their characteristics, not their own history — the stock page shows how much is prior.
  • Crude universe heuristics: ticker-pattern filters; some ADRs leak through; one share class per company.
  • First-order stress tests: exposure × shock, no convexity, no specific returns.
  • Full methodology and every parameter: docs/METHODOLOGY.md on GitHub.

The model in one paragraph

Barra-style fundamental factor model: 7 style factors (size, value, momentum, volatility, liquidity, quality, leverage) + 12 Fama-French industries + a market factor, estimated by weekly cross-sectional WLS regressions. Fundamentals and industries come from SEC EDGAR (public domain, point-in-time). Factor covariance is EWMA (13-week vol / 26-week correlation half-lives); specific risk is shrunk toward size buckets. Everything here is computed in your browser from the published artifacts — no server.
